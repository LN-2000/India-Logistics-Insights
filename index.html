The big picture

Logistics is more than distance.

India's logistics sector connects producers, markets and consumers across the country. Yet moving goods is not simply a matter of how far they travel. Transportation costs, infrastructure availability, business scale and the choice of transport mode all shape the economics of freight movement.

This analysis brings together published data to explore where cargo moves, what freight costs, why smaller businesses face a different cost burden, and when combining road and rail can make economic sense.

The central question: how can India's freight network balance cost, connectivity and service requirements?

India's maritime gateways

Where does India's cargo move?

Ports are critical gateways for domestic and international trade. Their cargo-handling activity and geographical distribution influence how goods enter, leave and move through the country.

A coastal network of gateways

Port activity is distributed across India's western and eastern seaboards, linking industrial clusters and hinterland markets to global trade routes.

Two-dimensional schematic of mainland India and the north-east, with major maritime port locations marked on the correct coastal regions. Marker positions are approximate; use your source data for exact port coordinates and cargo-volume bubble sizes.

Reading the port landscape

Port-level cargo distribution helps identify the concentration of maritime activity and the importance of key coastal regions in India's logistics network.

A port's significance is not determined by cargo volume alone. Hinterland connectivity, industrial proximity, vessel access and evacuation capacity also shape its role in the logistics system.

The economics of freight

The transportation cost dilemma.

Road, rail and waterways have different cost structures, capabilities and operational advantages. Average cost is an important starting point—but not the entire decision.

Average freight cost

Reported average transportation cost, ₹ per tonne-kilometre.

Source: NCAER, Assessment of Logistics Cost in India, September 2025. Values are reported averages, not fixed tariffs.

The lowest rate isn't always the lowest total cost

Rail and waterways appear more economical on a tonne-kilometre basis. But the complete logistics cost also depends on:

  • First- and last-mile road movement
  • Handling, loading and transshipment
  • Network connectivity and service frequency
  • Delivery flexibility and time sensitivity
Mode selection is a network decision—not simply a comparison of three rates.

Scale changes the economics

Smaller firms may have less negotiating power, lower shipment volumes and fewer options to optimise transport networks.

The gap between the smallest and largest turnover categories is 9.3 percentage points.

Larger businesses can often consolidate shipments, negotiate better rates and build more flexible distribution networks. These advantages can reduce logistics expenditure relative to output.

The multimodal advantage

When does rail become economically viable?

Rail's lower line-haul cost can be offset by road movement at both ends. The break-even distance therefore changes with first- and last-mile requirements.
